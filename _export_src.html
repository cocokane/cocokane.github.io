Yash Kokane

Ph.D. Student, Materials Science · Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute

I am a first-year Ph.D. student in computational materials science at Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute. My research uses density-functional theory to study the electronic structure of Weyl and topological semimetal thin films as candidates for next-generation interconnects. I am also interested in machine-learning methods that accelerate the discovery of new materials.

I.Research Interests

Electronic structure

First-principles (DFT) calculations of band structures, Fermi surfaces, and transport properties of quantum materials.

Topological semimetals

Weyl and Dirac semimetal thin films, where topologically protected surface states promise resistivity scaling superior to copper for back-end-of-line interconnects.

ML for materials

Machine-learned interatomic potentials and screening models that accelerate high-throughput materials discovery.

III.Projects

ML-driven optimization of Lennard-Jones parameters for MD simulations

Gaussian-process regression with genetic algorithms to fit force-field parameters, improving transport-property accuracy in Li-ion battery simulations.

Crack prediction in continuous steel casting

Industrial project with ArcelorMittal–Nippon Steel: random-forest feature analysis and RNNs on plant sensor data to identify crack-inducing casting parameters.

Sports biomechanics with computer vision

Custom YOLO detection and 3D pose pipelines (AlphaPose, MotionBERT) quantifying javelin-throw biomechanics from tournament video.
